THE JOEY

The Joey was designed and built by Keith Jarvis in Adelaide.
Between 1954 and 1970 he built three versions of the glider.


The first Joey

Keith built the first of the Joeys (VH-GFJ) in a small backyard shed at Torrens Park in Adelaide between 1950 and 1954. It was completed in 1955 weighing just 135 lb empty. Wing span 32 ft, wing area 80 sq ft, max speed 70 kts, stalling speed 25 kts, best glide 1/24, min sink 2.48 fps.

First flight was on January 11th, 1955 at Gawler, South Australia. Initial flight testing was done by Brian Creer at Gawler on April 1st 1955. In this article he goes into some detail about the flight and the characteristics of the glider.


On April 13 1956, in an attempt to better an endurance record, the glider was hand launched at Sellicks Hill south of Adelaide in gale conditions. The drawing at right shows notes of conditions and the path of the flight.

Details of the flight were published in the Adelaide Advertiser newspaper on 13th June 1956.



Another memorable event occurred in the late 1950s as related by his son, Ian.

Keith flew the Joey from Gawler on a whim across the city of Adelaide. He climbed to 10,000 feet and felt confident with cumulus ahead, but lost height nearing the coast and found himself at 1,000 feet eyeing Morphettville Racecourse during a race. Fortunately, he caught another thermal and continued.

A flight from Gawler to Sellicks beach is logged in a table of "Best flights" :
Feb 29 '56, Gawler to Sellicks beach, height 8500, 250 minutes.

The second Joey


The second Joey, the "J2", was built in a slightly larger shed at Plympton Park in 1968.

This had a one-piece cantilever wing, weighed 140 lbs and included a landing drag chute deployed from a tube inside the rear of the fuselage by a soda siphon CO2 cartridge activated from the cockpit. Short test flights were made at Gawler.

Unfortunately, despite having obtained a comprehensive stress analyis, Keith was unable to obtain approval for the design from the authority at the time, the "DCA" (Department of Civil Aviation) and after waiting more than 6 months he abandoned the project.



The third Joey

The third Joey VH-GNJ, an improved version of J1, was built around 1970 based on the existing approved strut braced design from the wings and tailplane of the J2 project. Weight 140lbs. First flight 11th November 1971.

On one occasion in the 1980's during a hang-glider competion in Burra, north of Adelaide, the glider was again hand-launched and Keith slope soared together with the hang gliders.

The glider was later fitted with a pylon mounted, converted 160cc Victa motor mower engine and registered with the RAAus in the 95:10 ultralight category. This proved quite successful with a good take-off and climb with a light pilot. Static thrust 55lbs, cruise 50kts. First flight was 7th April 1980.

Keith flew it frequently during the 1980s. The following video contains film taken at a flyin at Aldinga Airfield in 1982. It is part of a longer slideshow video of Keith's aircraft.

In the mid to late 80's the Joey was returned to the workshop for inspection and possible renovation as the Newsletter of the Vintage Glider Association of Australia in 1987 describes -

KEITH JARVIS WORKING ON OVERHAUL OF JOEY

Keith Jarvis of Adelaide has been very active in ultra-light aircraft activities for the past five or six years but now says "I have built my last one". A lot of his flying during these years has been in the diminutive Joey sailplane that he designed and built back in the 1950s. Most of this has been with a small engine fitted to the Joey and operating as an ultra light powered aircraft. Keith and his son Lynn have begun a major overhaul of Joey including recovering all the fabric parts. "So far," he comments, "we have found the structure quite sound despite some of it being made with urea glue." Keith is thinking of re-placing the original Victa motor with a McCulloch 100 cc motor to get more thrust. "If this works successfully I may rework the Joey fuselage to allow to motor to be re-tracted in flight," he says.


Other gliders

Other gliders were built around the same time together with the Joey.


From 1955 to 1957 Kevin Sedgman (left) built the 'Jumbuck' to Keith's revised design of the Joey and he was noted for his performances and aerobatics in it. First flights were on Oct 9th 1957.
Subsequently a complete flight test schedule as laid down for the issue of a Certificate of Type Approval was conducted by Brian Creer at Gawler. In his extensive report he goes into a great deal of detail about the flight characteristics of the glider.


The EP1 'Spruce Goose' was designed and built by Ted Pascoe in 1955.
Ted and Keith started building and encouraging each other when
they were both stationed at Parafield in 1953.

Glider history

Keith's log book notes that the original Joey J1 was sold on October 1958. It was advertised for sale in the 5th May 1957 issue of Australlian Gliding and in the 1959-03-March and 1960-03-March issues, Jack Herford is mentioned as the new owner. A 1963 accident with this aircraft was subsequently reported, along with reports for the other gliders. DCA Accident Reports 1956-1968.

Joey J1 28.09.63, Doctors Plains NSW Lakes Entrance VIC, PVT/Day.
The pilot allowed the glider to assume a very nose high attitude during an attempted launch with the result that it stalled and struck the ground before recovery could be effected. Damage substantial.

Keith notes that for the revised J2, numerous alterations gave more cockpit room and a better performance at high speeds. Kevin Sedgman built a glider to the J2 design and named it the "Jumbuck".
Accident history for this aircraft is recorded in the DCA reports.

Jumbuck J2 Glider 24.10.59. Townsite aerodrome Alice Springs NT, PVT/Day
The pilot attempted a steep turn at low altitude to land on the lee side of the aerodrome but lost control and the port wing struck the ground. Damage substantial.

No records have been found for the repair. The glider was returned to service and flown for another four years until in 1963 there was a second accident as reported in the DCA records.

GLH Jarvis J2 Jumbuck (glider) 24.11.63. Near Alice Springs Alice Springs NT, PVT/day.
A wing flutter in flight arose from extensive wear in the aileron cables and a substantial portion of the starboard mainplane was dislodged, whereupon the glider crashed out of control. Damage Substantial.

A subsequent comprehensive accident report was published in the March 1965 issue of Australian Gliding and this goes into considerable detail of the accident and cause.

The pilot was Mary Weaving. She was a resident of Alice Springs for 15 years, and the first woman in Australia to obtain the Silver C award in gliding, setting an Australian women's altitude record of 10,200 ft.
